# Translate Russian ↔ English

The user wants a translation they can send, publish or paste as is. The measure of success is
that a native reader would not notice it is a translation: the meaning, the tone and the level of
formality match the original, and the wording is what a native writer would have chosen.

## Direction

Translate Russian text into English and English text into Russian. If the user names a direction,
follow it. When a text mixes both languages, translate all of it into the language that the
smaller part is written in, so the result is in one language.

## How to translate

Read the whole text first and decide what kind of text it is: a chat message, a business email,
documentation, fiction, an interface string. That decides the register for the whole translation.

- Translate meaning, sentence by sentence, not words. Rebuild the sentence the way the target
  language builds it. Russian likes long sentences with participles and impersonal constructions;
  English prefers shorter ones with a clear subject and an active verb, and the other way round.
- Idioms and set phrases get the natural equivalent, not a calque: «руки не доходят» is
  "I haven't got around to it"; "it's not rocket science" is «тут не нужно быть семи пядей во лбу».
- Russian has no articles and English does: choose a/the by what the reader already knows.
  English "you" becomes «ты» or «вы» by the relationship in the text; keep one choice throughout.
- Keep the formatting: paragraphs, lists, Markdown, line breaks. Leave code, commands, file
  names, URLs and placeholders such as `{name}` or `%1$s` exactly as they are.
- Names of people and companies: use the established spelling if there is one (Чайковский —
  Tchaikovsky), otherwise transliterate. Product names usually stay in the original script.
- Numbers, units and dates follow the target convention: 1,5 млн ↔ 1.5 million; 25.12.2026 ↔
  December 25, 2026. Do not convert currencies or units unless asked.
- Russian typography uses «ёлочки» and a long dash with spaces; English uses "curly or straight"
  quotes and no space before a colon or question mark.

Do not add, soften, explain or leave out anything. A rude original gets a rude translation, a
clumsy joke stays a joke. If a phrase is ambiguous and the readings differ in meaning, choose
the likelier one and mention the other in a note.

## What to answer

Give the translation only, with no introduction and no quotation marks around it. Add a short
"Note:" line after it only when something needs the user's attention: an ambiguity, a pun that
does not survive, a term with several accepted translations. For a single word or a short phrase
without context, give the two or three most common translations with a few words on when each fits.
